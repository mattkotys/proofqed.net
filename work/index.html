Incoming inspection

How a machine shop could turn scattered receiving rules into one clear path from delivery to stock or hold.

The client’s process

Alder Bend Machining, a fictional job shop, buys machined components from several suppliers. One delivery can contain multiple supplier lots for the same purchase-order line. Receiving logs deliveries, Quality inspects parts, and Purchasing works with suppliers to obtain missing paperwork or arrange returns.

The purchase order identifies the part and revision. The inspection plan tells Quality which characteristics to check, which lots require a supplier certificate, and whether prior supplier issues call for closer inspection. Receiving has to piece those rules together across purchase orders, inspection plans, and hold records. A passing lot can move to stock while a second lot on the same delivery stays on hold.

Work instruction

Use one receiving record per supplier lot. Keep lots physically separate until each has a recorded outcome.

  1. Receiving logs the lot. Record the purchase-order line, supplier lot ID, part number, revision, and quantity received. Check labels against the packing slip and purchase order and look for visible damage. If anything differs or is damaged, follow the decision guide before proceeding.
  2. Receiving verifies the paperwork. Check the inspection plan for required documents. Match any required certificate to the part, revision, and lot. For missing or mismatched paperwork, hold the affected lot and ask Purchasing to obtain a correction.
  3. Quality confirms the inspection plan. Use the controlled plan for the received revision and check for supplier or part-specific inspection instructions. Hold the lot if the applicable plan or criteria are unclear. Quality resolves the gap before inspection proceeds.
  4. Quality inspects and records the result. Follow the approved plan and record the checks and results against the lot. If any check fails, create a nonconformance record and send the lot for Quality disposition.
  5. Receiving releases or holds the lot. Move it to stock only after Quality records approval. For any open issue, apply a hold label, move the lot to the hold area, and record its location, reason, and next owner.
  6. The next owner closes the hold. Once Purchasing obtains corrected paperwork, Receiving rechecks it and routes the lot back to Quality for any remaining inspection. For nonconforming material, Quality records the disposition and any required reinspection. Purchasing coordinates supplier returns when required.

Completion check: Each lot record shows where the material is, who owns the next action if any, and the release approval or disposition when closed.

Decision guide

Follow the checks for each lot. A hold applies only to the affected lot.

Read the decision guide as text

Follow the first applicable case for each lot. Other lots on the delivery continue through their own checks.

Identity, count, or condition is wrong
Receiving holds and records the discrepancy. Purchasing handles supplier questions. Quality evaluates damaged material.
Required paperwork is missing or mismatched
Purchasing obtains corrected paperwork. Receiving rechecks it before routing the lot back to Quality.
Inspection criteria are unclear
Quality confirms the applicable plan before the lot can be inspected or released.
An inspection check fails
Quality records the nonconformance and disposition. The lot stays on hold until that disposition is carried out.
All checks pass
Quality approves release. Receiving moves the lot to stock and records the location.

Two lots, one delivery

Two lots arrive against the same purchase-order line. The first has matching paperwork and passes inspection. It goes to stock after Quality approves release. The second has a certificate that names the wrong lot. It stays on hold while Purchasing gets a corrected certificate, then goes through inspection on its own record.

The work instruction puts the checks in order. The decision guide gives each exception a next owner and keeps a paperwork hold from stopping the entire delivery.
